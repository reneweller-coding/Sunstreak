# Sunstreak release notes

## Next (not released yet)

- **Drifting:** in a bend taken near the limit the nose turns in, the tail swings out, the outer rear tyre smokes and
  the body swings back at the bend's end. A drift of 30 m or more scores and raises the combo. Settings > DRIFT:
  OFF; LOOK ONLY (the car drives exactly as without it); or FULL (in a drift the car answers the wheel a little more
  and its sideways motion a little later, and the drift fills the boost tank). Three new awards.
- **Slipstream:** close behind a car in your lane the air holds you back less; overtake straight out of it for a
  SLIPSTREAM PASS.
- **The car:** tyre marks on the asphalt, brake lights (yours, the rivals', the traffic's), a body that dips braking
  and squats accelerating, wheels that turn and steer.
- **Houses on the ground:** everything beside the road now stands on the land as it is drawn. Houses on a slope get a
  terrace of earth and stand half in the slope, or move where it is too steep; fences and walls follow the slope;
  the trains' coaches follow their rails up and down.
- **The scenery:** soft shadows under houses, trees and parked cars; drives, hedges, fences and gardens in the
  villages; pavements, parked cars, cross streets with zebras and traffic lights and lit shop signs in the towns;
  forests of close-standing trees; rock above the tree line, snow above the snow line (lower in winter); the
  shadows of drifting clouds; banks of snow with a cut face and an uneven crest instead of straight walls.
- **Gentler roadsides:** met from the side, parked cars, garden hedges and walls and the banks of snow turn the car
  away like a guard rail instead of spinning it; a hedge driven straight into gives way.
- **Traffic:** it indicates before it changes lanes (not where the lines are a suggestion), and at night its headlights
  light the road ahead of it. **Flash the headlights** (F, or the left stick pressed in) and slower traffic in your
  lane makes way.
- **Traces:** ruts in snow, sand, gravel and the verge; dust, mud and slush on the car until the rain washes it; the
  longest drift on each legendary road is kept.
- **Life:** smoke from the villages' chimneys where it is cold, windows that light up at dusk, flocks of birds (gulls
  along the shore, vultures over dry land, crows elsewhere), a sea that is pale over the shallows with a line of surf.
- **Every country its own way, once more:** traffic lights (yellow heads over the road in the Americas, lying heads in
  East Asia, striped posts in South Asia, bordered heads at the kerb in Europe), crossings (two lines, yellow or white
  stripes, Belisha beacons in Britain), painted kerbs, what stands round a village plot (dry stone walls, pickets,
  dark wooden or painted plank fences, sticks, plastered walls), shop signs in the region's colours (and hung out
  from the fronts in East Asia), and what the country drives parked along its streets.
- Where the road runs between banks of snow, the land, the roofs and the trees are under snow too.
- **The horn** (H, or up on the d-pad), in the country's own sound: animals leave the road, and where the lines are a
  suggestion somebody answers. **Warning arrows** at the picture's edges for what is close beside the car or coming up
  fast from behind. Brakes that glow after long hard braking.
- **Sounds of the place:** cicadas in the heat of the day, crickets in the warm night, cowbells on Europe's high
  pastures, a church bell as the car comes through a village.
- **More that moves:** the wind turbines' blades turn, flags wave, boats ride the swell that now runs over the water;
  the rivals' and the everyday traffic's wheels turn; billboards carry the place's name in the country's own script.
- **Oncoming traffic** (Settings > ONCOMING TRAFFIC: OFF, LIGHT, NORMAL or DENSE; off at first): a road of two or
  four lanes is one of two directions, as its markings always showed it, and the half across the middle carries what
  comes the other way. Your own direction keeps to its half, so overtaking means going out there when nothing comes;
  an overtake on such a road is worth more (x1.25, x1.5, x2 by the level). What comes the other way flashes its lights
  and sounds its horn at a car in its lane, and it and the car you squeeze past keep to their edges to leave room in
  the middle. Missing it close and fast is a CLOSE CALL (+1000, times the combo); a red arrow at the top warns of what
  comes at you in your lane; meeting it is a head-on crash. On DENSE the oncoming traffic overtakes too, out in your
  half while it sees the way clear. A lorry or a bus going by shoves the car a little with its blast (and throws a
  sheet of spray in the rain). Its headlights show at night and in tunnels. Roads of three lanes stay one
  carriageway. At roadworks the other direction waits. The autopilot and STEER ASSIST overtake only with room, and
  not over a solid line. New award: HOLD YOUR NERVE (50 close calls; 73 in all).
- **The line down the middle** is solid where nobody sees far enough to overtake (round tight bends and over crests)
  and on four-lane roads.
- **Wet roads:** the lamps of the traffic lie mirrored in a wet road as streaks; the traffic throws up spray in the
  rain, slush in the snow and dust on gravel and sand; at night the traffic's tail lights glow.
- **The places' own shapes** face the road with their fronts; a funicular climbs the slope behind it; piers, jetties
  and the like stand where the land meets the water.
- The autopilot (and the assists) no longer moves over into a car beside it, the traffic looks in the mirror before
  it changes lanes in front of you, and BRAKE ASSIST also brakes for animals in the road.
- All vehicles' wheels turn now (the traffic's, the lorries', the police's), not only the everyday cars'.
- `--bench` also reports how the drive went (crashes, scrapes, time off the road, drifting, slipstream, what came
  the other way) and notes each crash with the traffic around it.

## 1.0.1

- **Reverse gear:** hold the brake at a standstill and the gear shows R; the brake then backs the car up (up to
  20 km/h, as far as 150 m), the throttle stops it and takes it forward again.
- **No more stuck in the verge:** off the road the car used to be held back so hard that, once stopped there (at a
  hairpin, say), it could never drive off again. Now it always crawls back to the road.
- Building from source: GCC 12 and the newest Visual Studio on GitHub's runners.

## 1.0.0

The first public release: an arcade racer round the whole world, for Windows, Linux and the Steam Deck.

- **The world tour:** 781 places in all 202 countries and territories, the big countries state by state, each with its
  landscape, landmarks, houses, roadside stands, wildlife, real climate, sun and smog. At the borders the road forks;
  between landmasses a glass tunnel on the sea floor, a ferry or the car train. The **random tour** (Settings >
  SCENERY) leaps to another part of the world after every stage, each country once a round.
- **Real heights:** the road climbs and falls between the places to their real height, from the Dead Sea to the
  Himalaya; 28 legendary roads, the passes climbing in numbered hairpins to the height on their sign.
- **Every country drives its own way:** side, markings, signs and place names in their own scripts, number plates,
  speed cameras, tolls, police, ambulances, traffic, road quality, poles, lamps and guard rails.
- **Life on the road:** hitchhikers and courier jobs, local heroes, police chases, fast trains, landing airliners,
  festivals, monsoon, snow and wet roads.
- **Modes:** the endless run against the clock, travel without a clock, the daily challenge, rallies and journeys, a
  championship against eleven rivals, the Track Lab with shareable codes and ghosts.
- **Sound:** thirty-one radio stations composing as you listen, engines of four, eight and twelve cylinders, your own
  music as the station MY MUSIC.
- **69 awards**, the passport with its map, stamps, regions, postcards and album, a manual of 16 chapters.
